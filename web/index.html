    On a key angle, the key drawing is shown exactly as drawn. Arrow keys and the key map land on key angles.
    Neuromation
    Latent rotation. Every drawing is a view of one latent object, and each was drawn from a slightly different vantage than requested (often turned a few degrees less, or seen from a little higher). That vantage is learned per drawing, and the drawing becomes a rigid shell: every point sits on the ray from its vantage at a smoothed depth, so the vantage sees it exactly as drawn and the shell can't shear or tear. Between keys the shell rotates; your angle maps to a latent angle that blends the nearby drawings' vantages. Depth relief sets how curved the shell is, from a flat card (no deformation at all) to the full smoothed shape.
    Walsh
    Walsh (sequency) interpolation, built on Neuromation. Both drawings either side of your angle are turned as rigid shells, with the rig's articulated hand-off, to the same in-between vantage. Each is split into Walsh/Haar sequency bands by box-averaging (the GPU's mip pyramid: additions only, no network). The low bands, her shape, colour and shading, interpolate smoothly with the blend proportion. The high bands, her line art, are never averaged (averaging them is what makes ghost double-lines): they hand over from one drawing to the other around the midpoint (Line hand-over sets how wide: narrow keeps lines crisp, wide flows continuously with faint doubling), with their spectral energy kept constant so lines don't fade as they hand over. Anything only one drawing has, like a ribbon drawn in a different place, is shown only while that drawing leads, so nothing is duplicated. On a key angle the bands sum back to the drawing exactly.
